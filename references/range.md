# Range: other fields, in terms of the four questions

Range supports Question 1 (what would a professional from another field add?) and Question 3 (which methods and tools do they use?). Range often produces the most unexpected results. Example from the essay: a 3D rendering tool from another field was used for an interior design.

## Question 1: what would a professional from another field add?

Replace nouns with verbs. The noun is the artifact. The verb is what it must make happen. Example from *The Art of Innovation* (Tom Kelley and Jonathan Littman): a quarterly review is not a deck. It is getting busy people to believe a story about cause and effect and to decide under uncertainty in thirty minutes.

The verbs show which fields can help. Then list the actions each field would take.

## Question 3: which methods and tools would they use?

For a deck that must be excellent, the actions a strong team would take are:

- Analysis of the raw data.
- Rigorous editing for clarity and accuracy.
- Motion and audio production, if the deck will be presented.
- Adversarial review: research the opposing case the way a short seller researches a position, and check the figures the way a forensic accountant would.

Carry out each action with its real method. Do not imitate a field's tone or style. The result is judged by what was done.

## Question 4: what would show it is right?

The adversarial review is the outside check for this range. Its findings must be tested against the raw data, not accepted on the review's word.

## Tools

Range requires access before it can start: software that can be installed, the relevant data, and connections to other systems. Set these up first, and write down what is still missing.
