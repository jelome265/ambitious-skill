# Background

People have always been held back by what they believe is possible, and usually trust themselves only within a narrow field.

Psychology calls this self-efficacy: people who believe in their own abilities tend to accomplish more, and research suggests the belief can be raised.

- Specific, hard goals outperform "do your best". Locke and Latham reviewed 35 years of goal-setting research and reached this conclusion.
- Believing something is possible makes it more likely. In 1939, George Dantzig copied two problems from a blackboard, thinking they were homework. They were two unsolved problems in statistics.
- Seeing someone else do it helps. Roger Bannister broke the four-minute mile in May 1954 and was the only person to have done so for 46 days.
- Permission to fail can improve results. *The Inner Game of Tennis* argues that trying very hard can reduce performance. Linux was announced as "just a hobby, won't be big and professional like gnu".
