# Background: why the four questions work

## What the essay says about human limits

People have always been held back by what they believe is possible, and usually trust themselves only within a narrow field. Michelangelo did not want to paint the Sistine Chapel because he was a sculptor, not a painter.

Psychology calls this self-efficacy. People who believe in their abilities tend to accomplish more, and research suggests the belief can be raised. The essay gives these examples:

- **Specific, hard goals.** Locke and Latham reviewed 35 years of goal-setting research and concluded that specific, hard goals outperform "do your best".
- **Believing it is possible.** In 1939, George Dantzig copied two problems from a blackboard, thinking they were homework. They were two unsolved problems in statistics.
- **Seeing someone else do it.** Roger Bannister broke the four-minute mile in May 1954 and was the only person to have done so for 46 days.
- **Permission to fail.** *The Inner Game of Tennis* argues that trying very hard can reduce performance. Linux was announced as "just a hobby, won't be big and professional like gnu".

## How the four questions relate (this skill's mapping, not the essay's)

The essay does not map these examples to the questions. The mapping below is ours:

- Specific, hard goals support Question 1: what would an expert aim for?
- Believing it is possible supports Question 1: an expert treats the difficult target as solvable.
- Seeing someone else do it supports Question 3: find the methods someone has already used.
- Permission to fail supports Question 4: test the result instead of protecting the first attempt.

The questions are there so the request is not limited by what seems possible at first.
